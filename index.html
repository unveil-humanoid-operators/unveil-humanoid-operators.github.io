Who Moved the Robot?
Humanoid Datasets Remember Their Operators

Sihat Afnan, Unnat Jain*, Habiba Farrukh*

*Equal contribution

TL;DR We show that human-to-humanoid motion retargeting preserves operator movement dynamics despite discarding body shape, enabling recovery of gender, age, height, and identity from robot joint-angle trajectories alone. Overview

Operator movement signatures survive the retargeting transform. Retargeting projects diverse teleoperators onto a uniform robot skeleton (Unitree G1), stripping body-shape parameters entirely. Operator-specific movement dynamics nevertheless persist in the joint-angle trajectories, and our proposed UNVEIL recovers the operator’s attributes directly from the humanoid data.

Abstract

Human-to-humanoid motion retargeting is the central operation in teleoperation data collection: it projects operator demonstrations onto a shared robot skeleton, discarding body-shape parameters, to produce training data for whole-body controllers and foundation models. Retargeting targets joint configuration, not movement dynamics. We report a surprising property of this transform: operator movement dynamics survive retargeting. On BONES-SEED (522 operators, 142K sequences), retargeted Unitree G1 trajectories support gender classification at 96.0% and operator re-identification at 97.2% Top-1; on operators never seen during training, gender holds at 83.4% and age, height, and weight regress with median absolute error of 2.1 yr, 3.9 cm, and 5.3 kg. Partial correlation analysis reveals emergent, biomechanically interpretable structure: the signals are task-invariant across activity categories and hold across retargeting implementations. We introduce UNVEIL, a spatiotemporal graph convolutional network to measure and interpret this effect, take initial steps toward operator anonymization, and ask what this implies for data practices and the operator-invariance of learned policies.

Inside UNVEIL: The GCN

UNVEIL overview. A retargeted humanoid trajectory is encoded into per-joint motion features, processed by two hierarchical spatial graph convolutions (intra-limb, limb-torso), aggregated across time with N spatiotemporal layers into a trajectory embedding that is decoded by task-specific heads into operator attributes.

Interpretable Features

Retargeting throws away body shape, but features of movement dynamics — how each operator's joints move through time — persist in the humanoid trajectory. That signal is enough to recover the operator's biometrics from the robot motion alone. The two panels below illustrate the effect for age and gender: each panel pairs two G1 demonstrations of the same activity by operators at opposite ends of the attribute, with the motion feature most predictive of that attribute plotted between them.
